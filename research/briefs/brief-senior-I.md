Your angle is PARITY OF EVERYTHING EXCEPT HAPPY-PATH DATA. Same subgraphs, same query, one through `GraphWeaver::Testing::Router`, one through the real gateway/router, and compare:
- **Errors.** A subgraph resolver raising `GraphQL::ExecutionError` on an entity field; on a root field; inside a list; with `extensions`; a subgraph returning `errors` with no `data`; a subgraph returning malformed JSON; a subgraph HTTP 500; a subgraph timing out (the real gateway has a timeout, the local router has `Failure.timeout` — compare the *shape* of what the client sees: `errors[].path`, `extensions.code`, whether `data` is partial or null). Where the local router differs, is the difference documented?
- **Null propagation.** Non-null entity field resolving to null across the seam; a non-null list element; a `@requires` prefetch returning null for the required field; `@provides` copy null where the owning subgraph has a value.
- **Headers and context.** The gateway forwards nothing by default; the local router has one context. A subgraph reading `context[:current_user]` vs a header: which spellings work in `:router`, `:wire`, and through the real gateway, and does the doc say which is the honest test.
- **Variables and directives.** `@skip`/`@include` with a variable on a field that is the `@key` crossing; a variable used in two subgraphs' arguments; a default value on a variable that only one subgraph sees; an `@defer` in the document (the gateway supports it; the local router should refuse by name).
- **Aliases and fragments.** The same entity field aliased twice with different arguments across the seam; a fragment spread on an interface across subgraphs; nested fragments with `__typename` at different depths; `__typename` on a `@requires` selection.
- **Introspection and `_service`/`_entities`.** Querying `_entities` directly through the local router (refused? passed through?), `_service { sdl }`, `__schema` through both.
- **Response ordering and extras.** Key order in the JSON, `extensions` from subgraphs (merged? dropped? by both?), a subgraph returning extra fields the query didn't ask for.
Report the matrix as local-router outcome / real-router outcome / same-or-differs / documented-or-not. Every "differs and undocumented" is a finding, ranked by how likely a test passing locally would fail in production.
