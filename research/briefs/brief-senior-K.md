# Senior K: the Apollo Router operator (production engineer)

Read /tmp/claude/graph_weaver/brief-senior4-common.md. Copy senior-app-C-fix to `senior-app-K`, log `senior-log-K.md`. You run the Rust Apollo Router in production in front of these subgraphs (install: `curl -sSL https://router.apollo.dev/download/nix/latest | sh`, senior I did it in a minute), and your question is: does a suite that passes under `graphql: :router` and `:wire` predict what my clients see through the real router with production config?

- **Persisted queries.** Configure the router for automatic persisted queries and then safelisted persisted queries (a manifest). Does the client send an APQ hash, can it, and what happens to a generated module when the router refuses a non-safelisted query — what error class, what message, what does `:wire` say about it. If the client can't do persisted queries at all, say what a team would have to do and whether the door is open.
- **Header propagation and auth.** Router `headers:` config forwarding `Authorization` to one subgraph only; `@requiresScopes`/`@authenticated` enforced by the router with a JWT plugin (or a coprocessor/rhai stub that rejects). What the client sees: error shape, `extensions.code`, and whether `Failure.graphql(code:)` can reproduce it in a spec.
- **Error redaction.** `include_subgraph_errors: false` (the production default): subgraph errors become a generic message. Does a suite written against `:router` (which shows full messages) mislead a team about what production returns; is there a mode or a `Failure` shape for the redacted form.
- **Entity and query-plan caching, `@cost` limits.** Router config with entity caching on one subgraph and a cost limit that rejects senior C's dashboard query: the rejection shape, and whether the client's retry logic ever retries a cost rejection (it must not).
- **Subscriptions and `@defer`.** A subscription over the router's multipart protocol and a `@defer` query: what the generated code does, what the transport does with a multipart response, and whether the refusal (if any) is at generation, at execution, or a parse failure.
- **Observability.** The router's tracing headers vs the client's `execute.graph_weaver` event: can a team correlate one request end to end; does the client forward a `traceparent` if asked (proc headers).
- **Operational drift.** The router hot-reloads a new supergraph; the app's checked-in dump is the old one: at what point does the client notice (a `verify`, a `schema:diff`, a runtime error) and is the message right.
