# Junior scenario 14: an app with no Sorbet

You are a Rails developer who has never used Sorbet and does not want to. Your team has a plain Rails app, no `sorbet`, no `tapioca`, no `# typed:` comments anywhere, and you want a typed-ish GraphQL client anyway because a colleague said graph_weaver's test fakes are good. Build a small Rails app at `/tmp/claude/graph_weaver/junior-app-14` whose Gemfile points at the gem checkout `/Users/dpepper/code/lib/ruby/graph_weaver` (path:) and does NOT include sorbet, sorbet-static, or tapioca. Toolchain: `~/.rvm/wrappers/ruby-3.4.9/bundle exec ...` (never `source rvm`). Do not modify the gem; it is read-only for you.

Follow README.md and docs/getting_started.md cold. Use the public Countries API (https://countries.trevorblades.com/graphql) as your remote graph. Do the install, write two queries and one with a variable, generate, run them in a request spec with `graphql: :fake` and once live, run `rake graph_weaver:verify` and `queries:check`, boot in `RAILS_ENV=production` and run `rails zeitwerk:check`. Then deliberately do wrong things and see what happens without a type checker: pass the wrong Ruby type as a variable, read a field you didn't select, index a nilable field without a nil check, misspell a prop.

Keep a log at `/tmp/claude/graph_weaver/junior-log-14.md` with timestamps: every place the docs or the tool assumed you had Sorbet (a `srb` command, a `# typed: strict` header you didn't ask for, a `sig` in an error message, a `T.nilable` in a doc you had to translate), every error message and whether it made sense to a non-Sorbet reader, and what protection you did get at runtime. Final message: minutes per step, the list of Sorbet assumptions with severity (blocked me / confused me / cosmetic), the runtime-protection findings, and one paragraph on whether you would adopt the gem without Sorbet. Do not fix anything in the gem; report.
